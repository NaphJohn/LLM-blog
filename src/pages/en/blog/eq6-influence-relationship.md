## 0. What This Chapter Solves

Chapters 1-5 form a progressive path from "stabilize self" to "deep connection." The capstone covers two things that make a relationship **sustainable**: (1) understand the mental shortcuts people use when deciding (influence), so sincerity lands better; (2) manage for the long run with the right approach — consistent small acts, predictable rituals, principled boundaries — not hot-and-cold. Core source: *Influence* (Robert Cialdini).

> A boundary first: knowing these principles is to **help sincerity land better**, never to manipulate. Using them to scheme others wins short-term and bankrupts long-term.

## 1. The Six Principles of Persuasion (and Their Relationship Translation)

- **Reciprocity**: give a small care first, the other naturally wants to return. → In a relationship: offer a specific small kindness first, instead of waiting to be cared for.
- **Commitment & consistency**: people tend to hold the stance they stated publicly. → Start from a low-threshold invite ("takes only half an hour, no pressure if busy"), let "willing" grow step by step.
- **Social proof**: people watch what others do. → Naturally show your normal social circle and the things you love; more attractive than self-promotion.
- **Liking**: people say yes to those they like. → Find similarity, give specific praise (see Ch.2).
- **Authority**: stable, professional, reliable builds trust. → Do your own things well; weighs more than verbal promises.
- **Scarcity**: fear of loss drives more than desire to gain. → Scarcity comes from **having principles and a life**, not hot-and-cold.

## 2. Long-Term Management: Three Counter-Intuitive Truths

- **Consistent small acts beat holiday sprints**: a small surprise every week, a "just picked this up" care, accumulates far more security than a Valentine's splurge. Echoes Ch.1's "small-points system."
- **Predictable small rituals feed security**: goodnight every day, a fixed weekend meet — predictability is the best antidote for anxious attachment (Ch.1), far more effective than dense bombing.
- **Boundaries = high-level scarcity**: a person with principles and their own life is actually more cherished. "Always available on call" without boundaries depreciates over time. Scarcity comes from "you are great, but you do not need me."

## 3. String the Six Principles into Daily Life

| Scene | Which principle | Concrete move |
|---|---|---|
| Want to ask her out | commitment + liking | low-threshold + specific + rejectable: "this shop takes half an hour, no worries if busy" |
| Want to be remembered | reciprocity + authority | remember a small thing she mentioned and quietly solve it; do your own things solidly |
| Want it to stay fresh | scarcity + social proof | hold boundaries and a life; naturally show your passions and circle |
