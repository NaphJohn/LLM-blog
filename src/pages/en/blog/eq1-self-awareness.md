## 0. Why the First EQ Lesson Is "Fix Yourself"

Many people study emotional intelligence to "fix the other person" — to make them love or listen more. But what actually determines relationship quality is whether **your inner frame is stable**. A person with clear boundaries, controlled emotions, and self-responsibility attracts others naturally, without tricks; conversely, no amount of "scripts" compensates for an inner collapse.

This chapter covers three blocks: (1) separation of tasks (stop carrying others' emotions), (2) attachment styles (see your default program), and (3) three daily, trainable stabilizing moves.

## 1. Separation of Tasks: Who Bears the Consequence Owns the Task

From *The Courage to Be Disliked* (Adler). The only test:

> **Whichever person ultimately bears the consequence of this matter owns the task.**

- You are responsible for: expressing yourself sincerely, holding your boundaries, and becoming the person you want to be.
- You are NOT responsible for: how the other person responds, whether they are happy, or what they think of you.

A lot of inner drain comes from exactly this — "She did not reply, did I do something wrong?" Whether she replies is her task (she bears the consequence: possibly missing a connection); you only own stating your piece clearly, then getting on with your life.

**Three landing moves**:
- *Do not interfere with the other's task*: do not demand instant replies, do not check up constantly, do not make decisions for her. Care is not control.
- *Do not carry the other's emotions*: her being unhappy is not proof you did wrong. Ask first: "Do you want me to keep you company, or help you think it through?"
- *Hold your own task*: make "what kind of person you are + how you express" solid — that is the only part you control, and the most attractive part.

> Separation of tasks is not coldness; it swaps "over-responsibility" for "each owns their own." The most exhausting relationships are usually one person carrying two people's tasks.

## 2. Attachment Styles: See Your "Default Relationship Program"

From *Attached* (Levine & Heller). Roughly 70% of the "drama" and "coldness" in intimate relationships does not come from a lack of love, but from unrecognized attachment styles:

- **Secure (about 50%)**: does not flee when vulnerable; treats "let us talk" as normal.
- **Anxious (about 20%)**: fears abandonment, oversensitive alarm system, prone to "testing" and "bombing".
- **Avoidant (about 25%)**: retreats into the cave under stress; the closer it gets, the more it wants to flee.

The classic death spiral is **anxious × avoidant** — one chases, one flees; the more one chases, the more the other flees.

**Key point**: attachment style is the brain's "default program," not a character flaw. People who state their needs clearly are actually the most valued.

- Rewrite "Are you getting sick of me?" (testing) into "I have missed you a bit lately, just want to check our rhythm is still OK?" (I-message).
- Use **predictable small rituals** (goodnight every day, a fixed weekend meet) to feed security, far better than密集轰炸 (dense bombing).
- When choosing a partner, look for "secure signals": does not flee when vulnerable, can talk feelings normally; do not misread "hot-and-cold heartbeat" as love.

## 3. Three Daily, Trainable Stabilizing Moves

- **I-message**: swap "You always..." for "I feel... when... because I need...". Accusation triggers defense; stating a need invites closeness.
- **6-second rule**: when triggered, count 6 seconds before speaking. The first 6 seconds of anger are the easiest window to say something wrong; ride it out and reason returns.
- **Catch emotion before solution**: when she vents, resist the lecture. Empathize once, then ask "Want advice, or want me to just rail with you for a bit?" — most of the time she wants to be held, not fixed.

> A handy identification tool: the **five love languages** (words of affirmation / quality time / receiving gifts / acts of service / physical touch). Know your own and your partner's primary language, state it directly — far more efficient than guessing.

## 4. Chapter Summary

- Separation of tasks = own only your task, do not carry others' emotions.
- Attachment styles = see your default program; use I-messages and predictable rituals to feed security.
- Stabilizing moves = I-message + 6-second rule + catch emotion before solution.

**In one line**: you do not need to become someone else to manage a relationship; you need to stabilize yourself first — next chapter: how a stable you gets naturally seen and liked.
