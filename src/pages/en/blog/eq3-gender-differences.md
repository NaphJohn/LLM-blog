## 0. What This Chapter Solves

Once a relationship turns daily, the most hurtful things are often not big events but "indescribable frictions": you give a solution when she vents and get called "not caring"; you go silent and she thinks "you stopped loving me." This chapter says: most frictions are not personality clashes, but **two sexes reading the same behavior under different rules.**

Core source: *Men Are from Mars, Women Are from Venus* (John Gray).

## 1. Seven Key Differences

| Dimension | Him (Mars) | Her (Venus) |
|---|---|---|
| Core need | trust / being needed | care / being valued |
| Under stress | retreat to the "cave" to recharge | talk it out, connect |
| Score system | big event = 100 points | every small thing = 1 point |
| Communication | solve first | be empathized first |
| Help signal | silence = fine | silence = not fine |
| Mood swing | like a rubber band, snaps back | like a wave, up and down |
| Expressing love | solve the problem | emotional attention |

> Key insight: **the root of conflict is not who is right, but that both sides interpret each other's behavior by their own planet's rules.** She wants "you heard me"; you want "the problem solved." Both are right, only the order is wrong.

## 2. Four Practices

- **Empathize before solving**: when she vents, say "I hear you," hold the solution, and ask "Want me to help think, or just rant with you?" — most of the time she wants to be caught.
- **Consistent small acts beat holiday sprints**: a small surprise every week (a kind word, quietly fixing a small annoyance) beats a luxury bag on Valentine's by ten times. The small-points system decides daily security.
- **Name and pre-announce "cave time"**: wanting solitude under stress is not cold violence. Say ahead "I am a bit tired, want quiet for an hour, then I am back" — turn "disappearing" into "predictable."
- **Letter technique**: do not fight in the moment when angry; write in four parts — angry → sad → afraid → what you truly want. Read it yourself once; the fire often halves, and you state the real need more clearly.

## 3. A Common Misread

"His silence = he stopped loving me" is a Venus misread; "her nagging = not independent enough" is a Mars misread. **Naming the difference** turns the script from "you changed" into "oh, your planet is speaking." A relationship that can say "Are you in your cave right now?" is easier than forcing mutual understanding.

## 5. Chapter Summary

- Cross-gender friction ≈ rule difference, not a character problem.
- Among the seven: he solves first / she empathizes first; his silence = recharge / her silence = SOS.
- Four moves: empathize before solving, consistent small acts, cave-time pre-announce, letter technique.

**In one line**: see the rule difference, and you rewrite the "you do not love me" script into "our planets just differ."
