## ★ Most Worth Your Attention Today

**The nastiest item in yesterday's 5-CVE PD-disaggregation storm — the Dumper RCE — is still unsettled a day later. Fix #44205 still lives only in main, the 0.5.23 tag predates it, and the advisories contradict each other. Upgrading to 0.5.23 is necessary but not sufficient.**

This is the only item today that can **outright breach your production deployment** — and the trouble is not the vulnerability itself, it is the **credibility and reachability of the fix stalling for a second day**.

Three layers of fact:

1. **The vulnerability**: `Dumper`'s `eval()` RCE, **CVE-2026-111391**, CVSS **9.2**.
2. **Conflicting advisories**: cybersecuretoday claims "**0.5.22+ is patched**"; Wiz / SentinelOne / VulnDB still list **0.5.10–0.5.22** as affected; and the actual fix PR **#44205** (replacing `eval` with an AST evaluator) **only landed in main, not in the 0.5.23 tag**.
3. **Impact**: you might think bumping to 0.5.23 yesterday closed it — but that tag predates the fix, so **0.5.23 does not equal patched**.

**Actionable conclusion**: if you run `Dumper` or are exposed to the public internet → **upgrade to 0.5.23 AND `unset DUMPER_SERVER_PORT`**, and the safest path is to **build from main**; stop ticking the box the moment the version number hits 0.5.23.

> Worth saying separately: this is the second straight day of a "**official and trackers disagree**" gray zone. It confirms a hard rule — your upgrade checklist must carry a line that reads "**independently re-verify the CVE status after upgrading**," not a ticket you close at version 0.5.23. When the fix PR sits in main and you installed a tag, the version number itself has stopped being a security credential.

### Under the Hood: vLLM's DSpark Block-Diffusion Speculative Decoding (Kimi-K3 day-0)

The most worthwhile technical read of the day — and the hard evidence that block-diffusion drafts are going mainstream.

**Structure**: a block-diffusion backbone + a **low-rank Markov correction head** + a **hardware-aware confidence head**; the single-step non-causal backbone proposes **8 tokens at once**, with block-level acceptance verification (`rejection_sample_method="block"`).

**Measurements (GB300 NVL72)**:

- Single-stream math reasoning **110 → 435 tok/s (~4×)**
- Aggregate throughput under concurrency up to **3.5×**
- 9-domain macro-average acceptance **4.11 tokens/round** (math 6.42, HumanEval 4.96)

**Cost**: you must train a **5B / 5-layer** draft model; it is **only effective in sampling config** — block verification fails under greedy.

> My read: block-diffusion drafts are moving from "concept" to "day-0 quantifiable dividend." But the 3.14× / 4× Kimi-K3 numbers carry prerequisites — they eat sampling, they eat a dedicated draft model, and they eat a big machine like GB300. Before porting this line to your own 7B / 70B, first confirm you are willing to pay the draft-model training cost and that your service runs sampling rather than greedy.

## 3. AI Papers & Industry Hotspots (Sources Present This Issue)

### Core Paper

**Flamingo** (DeepMind, arXiv:2204.14198, NeurIPS 2022) — proves that a **frozen 70B language model + zero-initialized gating + 1.5% new parameters** can grow "eyes": freeze the vision tower + freeze the LM, train only the **Perceiver Resampler** (64-latent fixed-length compression) and **GATED XATTN-DENSE** (tanh(α) gating, α zero-init), and ~1.5% of parameters take 16 multimodal tasks to few-shot SOTA. Core idea: fixed-length compression **decouples** vision cost from resolution; zero-init gating guarantees step 0 is equivalent to the original LM and won't break language ability; per-image masking lets "train on few images, test on many" generalize. Its few-shot ability is actually fed by **interleaved image-text web data** (dropping that path costs **17.3 points**). Impact: of 16 benchmarks, 6 beat fine-tune SOTA and all 9 few-shot benchmarks are refreshed (COCO CIDEr 84.3→113.8, VQAv2 56.3→67.6); "freeze the big parts + train the connectors" became the VLM standard template, with Resampler / gated injection still in use today (Qwen-VL, Helix, VLA visual-token budgets).

### Operator Explainers

**GRPO** (arXiv:2402.03300) | positioning: drop PPO's critic and use the **group normalization of G rollouts on the same question** as the advantage (A_i=(r_i−mean)/std), with KL estimated via **k3** straight into the loss. In-use models: DeepSeekMath / V3.2 / V4-family RL, Qwen post-training, TRL default G=8, RoboBrain 2.0's GRPO-RFT. Key points: resident model 4→3, GSM8K 82.9→88.2 / MATH 46.8→51.7, R1-Zero AIME 15.6→71.0; pitfalls = **all-correct / all-wrong groups collapse advantage to zero**, and KL cannot be folded into the reward.

### Performance Optimization

**ZeRO++** (arXiv:2306.10209) | positioning: ZeRO-3's **3M** communication is too expensive, so **qwZ (INT8 weights) + hpZ (in-node replica, cross-node all-gather zeroed) + qgZ (INT4 gradients, reduce in-node first then cross-node, full-precision accumulate on arrival)** compresses it to **0.75M**. Representative gains: 384-GPU GPT-3-class at **45%+ peak compute**, up to **2.16×** (2.4× across the 10–138B range), low-bandwidth clusters catch up to 4× the bandwidth; MixZ++ LoRA **3.3×**. Embodied tie-in = VLA distributed fine-tuning across multi-site, low-bandwidth clusters.
