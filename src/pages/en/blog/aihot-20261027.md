## ★ Most Worth Your Attention Today

**SGLang's unpatched session-replacement DoS (CVE-2026-110809): a single request can take down your production, and the official fix isn't merged yet.**

This is the only item today that will **outright crash your production service** — unlike last issue's NVFP4 endless repetition, this is an **availability attack surface**, not an accuracy problem.

Three layers of fact:

1. **Symptom**: when `replace=true` is sent without an `rid` field, it triggers a **SIGQUIT crash**. An attacker (or even just a miswritten client) can take down your inference node with one crafted session-replacement request.
2. **Status**: disclosed 10-15; the fix PR **#41483 is not yet merged**. As of today, there is **no released fix on main**.
3. **Mitigation**: until the official fix lands, production must **restrict port exposure + enforce API auth** at the boundary.

**Actionable conclusion**: if you expose SGLang on a public or untrusted network, **confirm right now** that the gateway enforces auth and the inference port is not directly reachable from outside. This is not something "the next tag" will solve — during the window you can only rely on your own perimeter defense.

> Worth saying separately: a window where "**disclosure precedes the fix**" is more dangerous for inference services than for training — inference nodes face requests directly, with a large attack surface and a high restart cost. Writing the "temporary perimeter policy when a CVE drops" into a runbook pays off far more than firefighting after the fact.

### Under the Hood: Load-Aware Speculative-Decoding Length Control (drafters-under-load)

The most worthwhile tuning read of the day — especially for DFlash/DSpark deployments.

**The phenomenon**: EAGLE-4 / DFlash / DSpark yield **2.6–3.6× at batch=1**, but when concurrency climbs to **512**, they actually come in **below vanilla** (14.2K / 16.6K vs 21.2K tok/s). Reason: under batching the GPU is already compute-busy, so verification overhead becomes a net contest.

**The numbers**: Liquid DSpark decays to only **1.71× at 256 concurrency**; Meta Muse Spark Open therefore **hard-codes a 7-token draft** (suffix decay: acceptance rate 87.5% at token1 → 39.5% at token6).

**Takeaway**: **DFlash/DSpark deployments must attach a load-aware controller that dynamically tunes draft length** — otherwise high concurrency makes things slower. For OpenInfer / DFlash / DSpark this is a direct tuning signal: don't treat draft length as a constant.

> My read: speculative decoding is past its honeymoon. In the single-user era it was a guaranteed win, but once it enters production's high-concurrency world, "whether to speculate, and how many tokens" becomes a question that needs to be decided online. The next competitive front will shift from "whose drafter is faster" to "who can get the draft length right under load."

## 3. AI Papers & Industry Hotspots

The papers-and-industry daily ran normally today; here are the highlights.

### Paper Core: InstructGPT's Three-Stage RLHF

**InstructGPT** (OpenAI, arXiv:2203.02155, 2022) — turned GPT-3 from "text-completion" into "an instruction-following assistant," the direct precursor to ChatGPT.

- **Three stages**: ① annotator demonstrations for **SFT** to set the behavior baseline; ② ranked comparisons to train a **6B reward model** (Bradley-Terry pairwise loss — ranking is more reliable than scoring); ③ **PPO + per-token KL penalty (β=0.02)** to prevent reward hacking, with PPO-ptx to prevent capability forgetting.
- **Impact**: **a 1.3B InstructGPT beat a 175B GPT-3 in human evaluation (roughly 135× parameter efficiency)**; generalizes to unseen prompts; defined the default training paradigm for dialogue models and is the root of the DPO/GRPO lineage. Embodied extension: RoboBrain GRPO-RFT, π*0.6 advantage conditioning.

> This "back to the alignment roots" recap is worth rereading often: all of today's DPO/GRPO profusion grew from two plain judgments — "ranking is more reliable than scoring" and "use a KL penalty to stop drift." The parameter-efficiency line (1.3B > 175B) remains one of the most persuasive pieces of evidence for small-model alignment to this day.

### Operator Deep-Dive: DSpark Confidence Scheduling + Semi-Autoregressive Drafting (DeepSeek×PKU, arXiv:2607.05147)

- **Positioning**: in parallel drafting (DFlash) tokens within a block have no dependencies → suffix acceptance decays; DSpark uses a **Markov low-rank logit bias (rank=256)** to add dependencies + a **BCE confidence head** to predict survival probability + **hardware-aware prefix scheduling** to set verification length dynamically.
- **Models in use**: DeepSeek-V4-Flash (single-user +60~85%), V4-Pro (+57~78%), the entire Qwen3 family (acceptance length +26.7~30.9% over EAGLE-3); built into vLLM speculators, with a GLM-5.2 speculator preview.
- **Key points**: online verification 5 → 3.2~4.4 (-30%) with +6% throughput; 2 layers beat 5-layer DFlash; DeepSpec released MIT-licensed, full stack.

### Performance Optimization: SWA Bounded Replay Sliding-Window Replay (DeepSeek V4.1-Flash / DeepInfer)

- **Positioning**: the CED-architecture decoder lacks prompt-local SWA state; exact reconstruction needs the trailing 2560 tokens × 20 layers; **only the most recent 128 tokens are replayed**, at a fixed cost independent of context length.
- **Gains**: token reduction **20×**, prefill nearly halved, persistent KV footprint = **1/8** of V4-Flash, global KV **890 B/token** (1/437 of V1). SWA KV sits in host DRAM with a short TTL (no SSD writes); global KV persists ≥72h + FP4.
