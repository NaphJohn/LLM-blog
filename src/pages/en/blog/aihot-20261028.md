## ★ Most Worth Your Attention Today

**vLLM pushes the KV cache down to 4 bits (NVFP4): ~4× context per GPU, rewriting the VRAM ledger for long-context serving.**

This is the only item today that will **change your provisioning spec** — not a bug, but a downward shift in the cost structure.

Three layers of fact:

1. **What**: in v0.33.0rc0 (tagged 10-26, anchored on the MRV2 adaptive-verification cost-analysis fix #63431) vLLM rolls out low-precision KV broadly — NVFP4 4-bit covers SM8x/SM12x/SM90/SM100; GLM-5.x defaults to FP8 KV (+2.3~5.5% throughput), GLM-5.3-Flash SM90 +31%.
2. **The magnitude**: KV grows linearly with concurrency × context, so **4-bit gives ~4× context per GPU**. A long-context agent session idling in the background now occupies one quarter of the memory.
3. **The cost**: 4-bit has a range of only ±6 and requires **per-layer calibration** (amax/6.0); calibrated right it is near-lossless with 4× density, calibrated wrong it drops quality. vLLM reuses the fp8-KV llm-compressor calibration + compressed-tensors serialization; vo_split splits a 512-wide head into two 256 passes (measured error 0.0047 on a 5090).

**Actionable conclusion**: for long-context / multi-tenant setups, **upgrade once v0.33 is stable** — together with MRV2 elastic EP hot-scaling (on-demand MoE compute) it cuts the marginal cost of "long context is expensive." But during rc0, **production stays pinned to v0.32.0**, and run the calibration through the fp8-KV pipeline first.

> Worth saying separately: KV quantization was never just "precision for VRAM." Its real lever is **raising the physical ceiling on context capacity by 4×**, which changes how much concurrency and how many cold sessions you can hold. This is infrastructure-level "scale capacity without scaling cards" — more elegant than just piling on VRAM. But precisely because it touches the VRAM ledger, calibration quality decides whether you drop points in production — don't ship the default calibration straight to prod.

### Under the Hood: Why NVFP4 4-bit KV Can Stay Near-Lossless

The most worthwhile technical read of the day — especially before you take on long-context load.

**The path**: KV tensor ×(amax/6.0) scaled to ±6 → 4-bit quantize → serialized with compressed-tensors; dequantized back to compute at inference. 4-bit density is 4× that of BF16, so "~4× context per GPU" is a physical result, not a tuning illusion.

**Calibration is the crux**: take per-tensor amax and calibrate, and it stays near-lossless; vo_split splits a 512-wide head into two 256 passes, with only 0.0047 error on a 5090 — showing the path holds even on consumer cards. The failure cost is obvious output degradation that **throws no error**, so calibration must be validated first via the fp8-KV llm-compressor pipeline.

> My read: KV quantization is the key to "democratizing long context," but it is **calibration-driven**. If nobody on your team owns "pre/post-quantization accuracy regression" yet, now is the time to name someone — alongside tiered KV and PD disaggregation, this is turning the inference engine into a true "state-scheduling runtime."

## 3. AI Papers & Industry Hotspots

The papers-and-industry daily ran normally today; here are the highlights.

### Paper Core: SayCan (the First Grounding from LLM to Robot)

**SayCan** (Do As I Can, Not As I Say, Google, arXiv:2204.01691, CoRL 2022) — the first time "what the LLM says" and "what the robot can do" were joined by multiplication, the foundational bridge of the VLA lineage.

- **Core mechanism**: p(c_i|i,s,l_π) ∝ P(can|s) × P(say|i) — the LLM supplies "what to do" (semantic score), the RL-learned value function supplies "whether it's doable" (affordance score), the two probabilities are multiplied per skill and argmaxed, looping to Done. The Can side is trained with sparse rewards {0,1}, so its output is naturally a success probability.
- **Data**: a skill library of 551 / 7 families, BC-Z 68000 teleop demonstrations + the MT-Opt value function.
- **Results**: 84% planning / 74% execution on 101 tasks; the No-VF ablation drops to 67%; of errors, 65% trace to the LLM and 35% to affordance.
- **Impact**: the two-stage "planner + skill library" was later end-to-end-ified by PaLM-E / RT-2 / VLA; the VF idea was revived in π*0.6 RECAP — **affordance is the unavoidable foundation from LLM to action**.

> This "back to the grounding roots" recap is worth rereading: all of today's flourishing end-to-end VLAs grew from the plain multiplication of "semantic score × affordance score." The hardest leap in embodied intelligence is not making the model speak prettier, but making every step it speaks actually "doable."

### Operator Deep-Dive: The Rejection-Sampling Acceptance Criterion (the Lossless Base of Speculative Decoding)

- **Formula**: accept = min(1, p(x)/q(x)), and on rejection resample from norm(max(0, p−q)); min(p,q) + max(p−q,0) = p — one line proving the output distribution exactly equals the target model.
- **Models in use**: DeepSeek V3/V4 MTP, Qwen3 deployments, EAGLE, JetSpec, DSpark **all share this verification kernel**.
- **Key points**: ① resampling q after rejection is a lossy implementation (the biggest pit — always recover the leftover mass on the p side); ② the expected acceptance rate α sets the speedup ceiling and closes the loop with the JetSpec formula; ③ embodied tie-in — **the VLA edge-side acceptance rate α sets the control-frequency ceiling**.

### Performance Optimization: Prompt Lookup Decoding

- **Positioning**: look up the just-generated trailing n-gram inside the prompt, copy the following k tokens wholesale as the draft + verify with the standard acceptance criterion — **zero training, zero draft head, zero extra VRAM**.
- **Representative work**: built into HF transformers; vLLM `--speculative-method ngram`; the same in SGLang.
- **Gains**: ~**2×** on copy-style tasks (summarization / code editing / RAG), near 0 on open-ended creation; complementary to DSpark (ready-made draft from input vs. model-learned draft).
