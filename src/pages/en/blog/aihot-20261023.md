### Under the Hood: vLLM Push-Based NIXL KV Transfer — removing the "serial round-trip" from the critical path

- **Approach**: in pull mode, router-forward → decode-allocate → NIXL READ is serial; push mode registers the target block with prefill at allocation time, and prefill pushes KV over a background thread right after computing it, so decode no longer waits for that round-trip.
- **Measured**: 1P1D P5en+EFA Qwen3-32B up to 3× TTFT (1744→540ms @8QPS/16k), ~30% TPOT (10.4→7ms).
- **Boundary**: lowers latency only, not throughput; needs long prompts (8k+) + high concurrency + RDMA; worst-case disables prefix cache — meaning it is mutually exclusive with prefix-hit optimization, so settle the workload profile before deploying.

## 3. AI Papers & Industry Hotspots

### Today's Highlight (1 sentence)

**DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash uses a "causal encoder-decoder" to split prefill and decode into two workloads (8B / 16B asymmetrically activated) and packs global KV to 890 B/token — this is not a memory-saving trick, it writes "a robot is an extremely read-heavy, write-light AI user" into the network topology.**

### Paper Core (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash · Causal Encoder-Decoder)

- **One-line positioning**: 40 layers split into 20 causal encoder layers + 20 decoder layers, activating only 8B at prefill and 16B at decode — making "allocate capacity by workload" an architectural hard constraint.
- **Core idea (3 lines)**:
  1. the decoder's global KV is projected once from the encoder's final hidden state and shared across the 20 layers, instead of being built layer by layer;
  2. **CSA2** statically assigns each layer one of Full/Reindex/Reuse modes, the main KV and Top-K index are reused across layers, and a tiered index decouples deep-layer cost from context length;
  3. **SWA Bounded Replay** replays only the most recent 128 tokens, pinning the recompute cost to a constant.
- **Result / impact**: global KV at **890 B/token** = 1/4 of V4-Flash, 1/437 of V1, just 0.83 GB at 1M context; "allocate capacity by workload" becomes a new design dimension beyond the Transformer — directly echoing this author's DFlash/DSpark block-level KV scheduling.

### Operator Deep-Dive: FP4 / MXFP4 Block Floating-Point Quantization

- **Positioning**: compress weights/KV to 4 bit, doubling both GEMM and bandwidth on Blackwell tensor cores, recovering dynamic range via block floating-point scaling (E2M1 elements + one E4M3 scale per 16 elements).
- **In-use models**: DeepSeek V4.1-Flash (main KV, one E4M3 scale per 16 channels), DeepInfer/DeepGEMM, NVIDIA Blackwell (MXFP4/NVFP4 hardware-level), Qwen3 on-device Flash variants.
- **Key point**: effective bit-width 4+8/B (B=16 → 4.5 bit), measured compression 56.2% rather than 50%; fundamentally differs from INT4 per-group in that the scale itself is floating-point, adapting to extreme ranges.

### Performance Optimization: Layer-Shared KV Cache

- **Positioning**: decode is memory-bound, reading every layer's KV at each step; let adjacent layers share one copy, dropping "KV-read layer count" from L to f (gain=L/f).
- **Representative work**: CSA2 three modes + tiered index (V4.1-Flash); **MLIPS** (arXiv:2610.27438, 1.14–1.40×, GPU energy −14.8~−27.4%); **MOSAIC** (arXiv:2607.09029, prefill 1.76× / decode 2.54×).
- **Benefit**: multiplicatively stacks with FP4 and non-disk SWA to reach the 1/4 KV cost; over-deep index sharing drops task accuracy.
