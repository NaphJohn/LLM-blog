## 0. What This Chapter Solves

Chapter 1 stabilized your inner frame. This chapter asks: once a person is stable, how do they **naturally get seen and liked** — and not through looks or talent, but through a set of learnable behaviors.

Core source: *How to Make Anyone Fall in Love with You* (Leil Lowndes). Its counter-intuitive conclusion: **attraction is a trainable set of behaviors, not mysticism.**

## 1. The Six-Stage Attraction Model

Lowndes splits "from stranger to lasting love" into six progressive stages, each with concrete actions:

1. **First impression**: the "felt-seen" feeling in the first few seconds sets the tone.
2. **Similarity**: people fall for those who resemble themselves.
3. **Self-support**: when you are well and self-accepting, others are drawn to your energy.
4. **Build intimacy**: via a rising spiral of self-disclosure.
5. **Maintain love**: consistent small acts beat one-off grand gestures.
6. **Hunting strategy**: actively enter high-value circles so frequency meets naturally.

One-line core: **attraction = showing up where the other needs + expressing it in a language they understand.**

## 2. Five Actions You Can Practice Immediately

- **Pupil gaze**: on first meeting, hold eye contact on the pupils for about 3 seconds, then slowly look away. This triggers the other's "being-noticed loop," closer than darting eyes.
- **Echoing**: repeat a keyword from their sentence, then open your own feeling ("You also like diving? I once..."). The cheapest way to build "same-frequency" rapport.
- **Precise praise**: praise a **specific behavior**, not a vague compliment. "You helped that auntie pick things up earlier, that was thoughtful" beats "you are great". Formula = observe detail + infer character.
- **Small-dream fulfillment**: quietly deliver on a wish they mentioned offhand within 48 hours ("just picked this up"). The lowest-cost, highest-impact expression of the "acts of service" love language.
- **Gradual self-disclosure**: share a small vulnerability first; once they catch it, go deeper. Reciprocal layering builds real trust. Dumping everything at once scares people off.

## 3. Amplify Fondness with the Liking Principle

The **Liking** principle from *Influence* is especially handy at first contact:

- **Similarity**: people like those like themselves. Find common ground (same city, same hobby, same struggle) and fondness ticks up naturally.
- **Precise praise**: see above. The more specific, the more it shows you actually "see" them, not reciting lines.
- **Cooperation not competition**: frame the interaction as "we do this together," not "me vs you."

> Mind the boundary: Liking helps **sincerity** land better, not disguise. Fabricated similarity falls apart the moment you go deeper.

## 4. Put "Being Seen" in the Right Scenes

Attraction is not hunting by pickup lines; it is **placing yourself in the right scenes so frequency meets naturally**:

- Interest communities (ultimate frisbee / book clubs / climbing / improv), professional growth events, sports socials, volunteering, friends-of-friends (six degrees).
- After 3-5 online common-ground chats, move offline fast — relationships grow warm only through real interaction.
- High-value circles are "attracted into," not forced in: give value first, be a giver not a taker.

## 5. Chapter Summary

- Attraction = trainable behavior, not a gift.
- Five moves: pupil gaze, echoing, precise praise, small-dream fulfillment, gradual self-disclosure.
- Liking principle: similarity + precise praise + cooperative stance, let sincerity be seen.

**In one line**: stabilize yourself first (Ch.1), then break "being liked" into practice (this chapter) — next chapter: when a relationship turns daily, where those "indescribable frictions" between the sexes actually come from.
